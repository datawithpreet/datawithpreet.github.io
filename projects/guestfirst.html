08 · Restaurants · Customer Intelligence · Agentic AI

A Great Restaurant Fixes the Order. An Exceptional One Fixes the Experience.

What if a restaurant could connect customer feedback, order history, kitchen operations, and loyalty data to understand what went wrong — and coordinate the right recovery? GuestFirst AI identifies service issues, recommends personalized responses, and tracks follow-up so restaurants can turn unhappy customers into an opportunity to rebuild trust.

Real-world proof: Chick-fil-A tested geofencing and predictive order wait times at 100 restaurants. Customers' wait times decreased by an average of one to two minutes, while estimated wait times were accurate more than 90% of the time — data improving hospitality, not replacing it.
QSR Magazine: Chick-fil-A to Deploy Geofencing Technology ↗

The business outcome

Most systems collect feedback. GuestFirst connects feedback to operational evidence — order data, kitchen timing, loyalty history — and helps coordinate what happens next. The outcome: every complaint becomes an investigated case with a proposed recovery, a manager decision, a drafted customer message, and a 48-hour follow-up check.

The differentiator is the interactive loop: the agent thinks out loud, investigates, and proposes — then the manager approves, adjusts, or rejects. Recovery stays a human decision, informed by evidence.

How the agent works

A free-text complaint box (with example chips) starts a real interactive loop — investigate → propose → approve → message → follow up:

1
Listen

Takes the guest's complaint in free text — cold food, wrong order, long wait — and parses the issue type.

2
Connect

Pulls the order record, kitchen ticket timing, and pickup records to find operational evidence behind the complaint.

3
Diagnose

Identifies the likely root cause — a kitchen delay pattern, a recurring wrong-item error, a demand surge overwhelming the grill queue — thinking out loud so the manager sees the reasoning.

4
Propose

Recommends a recovery action from the policy-approved playbook, proportionate to the issue.

5
Manager decides

Approve / Adjust / Reject — nothing goes to the guest without a human decision.

6
Message + follow up

Drafts the customer message and schedules a 48-hour follow-up to verify the issue was resolved.

The research grounding: a fast-food service study found courteous, attentive service correlates most with satisfaction — the "why the recovery matters" section of the demo puts the manager in a second-person role-play of that finding.

Data engineering

The demo runs on a synthetic restaurant dataset: complaints, order records, kitchen ticket timings, and loyalty histories — including the Friday-lunch rush scenario (34 complaints → pattern-chosen inspection → mechanism-tested root cause → staged recovery). Feedback is joined to operational evidence the way a real customer-data platform would join it.

Implementation

  • Deterministic: stdlib only, offline — identical runs; edge-case batteries in TEST-LOG-EDGE.md.
  • Honest boundaries: a proposed demonstration inspired by documented restaurant practices. The Chick-fil-A sources do not establish that it uses this exact agentic workflow — the page and demo say so explicitly.
  • Sidebar research: the demo's learn-more sidebar leads with the Hightouch piece on how Chick-fil-A turns guest data into caring experiences.

Technical details

  • Run it: python3 app.py, then open http://localhost:8008 in Chrome and click Run live demo (binds 127.0.0.1, port 8008).
