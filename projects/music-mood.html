Your Next Song Should Fit Your Moment — Not Just Your Listening History.

Music can help shape how we feel, focus, and unwind. This agent combines a small session memory with your stated mood and chosen occasion to recommend music for the moment.

Real-world proof: Spotify reported that a conversational recommendation experience increased additional user listening by 14% and reduced skip rates by 5% in a two-week A/B test involving approximately 15 million users. These are engagement results — not proof of improved mood or productivity.
Spotify Research, September 2026 ↗

The business outcome

Recommendation engines optimize for the next click. Nobody builds the sequence: an arc with a beginning, a turn, and a landing — plus the honesty of saying why each song fits, and a memory of what worked for you last time. The outcome this project delivers: a playlist built as an emotional arc — MATCH the mood you're in (so it starts honest, not forced) → LIFT you toward where you want to be → SUSTAIN the good mood once you're there.

Mood is not reliably inferred from time or location alone. This demo treats contextual signals as clues, prioritizes your stated mood, and makes sensitive data optional.

How the agent works

Three agents, each printing its PLAN and numbered steps so you can see the agentic loop — interpret → target arc → retrieve → score → sequence → explain:

1
Interpret

Reads your plain-words request ("I'm stressed after a long workday") plus your chosen occasion (studying, workout, road trip…) — and infers the current mood and the target mood. Keyword-based mood detection; no black box.

2
Design the arc

Plans the emotional sequence: MATCH (meet you where you are) → LIFT (move you toward the goal) → SUSTAIN (hold the good mood). Every arc slot becomes a retrieval query.

3
search_catalog

Filters the track catalog per arc slot on mood tags, energy, and valence (tempo shows up in each why-it-fits line). Every pick comes from the real catalog through this tool — never hallucinated by an LLM.

4
Score and sequence

Ranks candidates per slot; tracks that worked in past sessions get a [MEMORY-BOOSTED] weight (+8 points) the next time you ask for the same goal.

5
Explain

Presents the playlist with one why-it-fits line per song — the honesty layer recommendation engines skip.

6
Adapt

In a production build, skips, likes, or a changed mood would feed back into the next recommendation; in this demo, session memory is pre-seeded, not learned live.

Three situations play back-to-back in the demo: stressed after work, road trip with friends, can't focus while studying.

Data engineering

tracks.json holds 35 real, recognizable Spotify tracks — every track ID verified through Spotify's oEmbed, with inline lazy-loaded 30-second preview players (no login needed). Each track carries approximate teaching values for energy, valence, and tempo — labeled as teaching values, not measured audio features. Session memory stores which tracks worked for which goals and biases future picks.

The differentiator was inspired by real listening-data analysis: listening patterns follow mood patterns across time of day and activity — which is exactly what this agent automates.

Architecture

LayerWhat it does
Interpreter agentParses the request + optional context into current mood and target mood; prints its PLAN.
Arc designerBuilds the MATCH → LIFT → SUSTAIN sequence and turns each slot into a retrieval query.
Retrieval toolsearch_catalog — every song pick comes from the catalog via this tool; you see every tool call.
MemoryPast sessions bias future picks ([MEMORY-BOOSTED]); feedback (skips, likes, mood change) re-plans the arc.
ExplainerOne why-it-fits line per song — transparent reasoning, not a black-box queue.
Web UIapp.py over stdlib http.server; no installs, no API keys, fully offline.

Implementation

  • Deterministic: stdlib only, offline, no randomness — identical runs; TEST-LOG.md records the determinism gate, TEST-LOG-EDGE.md the edge-case battery.
  • Honest boundaries: mood detection is keyword-based; track audio values are teaching approximations; this is entertainment, not therapy.
  • Why an agent, not a chatbot: an LLM would hallucinate song titles. This system plans, uses tools, reasons over multiple steps, and remembers — the structure is the point.
