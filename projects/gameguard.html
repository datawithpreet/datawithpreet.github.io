Bad Actors Don’t Always Use Bad Words. Can AI Recognize the Pattern?

From suspicious links and requests for personal information to manipulative conversations with young players, harmful behavior can hide behind emojis, slang, misspellings, and seemingly innocent messages. GameGuard AI analyzes messages and conversation context to flag potential grooming, scams, violent threats, and fraud for appropriate review.

Real-world proof: Supercell's games reach around 290 million monthly active players — a community scale that makes player safety a massive operational challenge, and the reason Supercell's Trust & Safety team applies machine learning to detect and prevent player harm.
How Supercell Uses Databricks to Serve Nearly 300M Monthly Players and Prevent Player Harm ↗

The business outcome

Traditional moderation asks, "Does this message contain a prohibited word?" GameGuard asks, "What might be happening across this interaction, and does it warrant intervention?" The outcome: fewer real harms buried in a backlog of false positives — flag-everything systems drown moderators in noise, and at platform scale a small false-positive rate is a backlog that buries the real P1 cases.

The old keyword filter flagged the word "password" wherever it appeared — including when a player warned others about a password scam — and missed the actual scammer, because the scammer never said anything offensive. He said "FREE gems 🎁", then "send me your password 🔑", in three friendly messages spread across an afternoon.

How the agent works

An orchestrator streams its plan and tool calls into an agent-trace panel, with case outcomes, evaluation metrics, and a session summary in cards below:

1
Read in context

Ingests messages with conversation history, emojis, slang, and misspellings — in three languages — instead of judging single messages.

2
Detect signals

Suspicious links, personal-information requests, grooming patterns, violent threats, fraud — scored as signals, not verdicts.

3
Correlate across conversations

Connects repeated behavior across conversations and correlates chat with transaction history — the patterns keyword filters structurally cannot see.

4
Two-tier escalation

Bare violent trash talk is logged but not auto-escalated; real-world credibility anchors (address, school, weapon context) escalate P2 to trust & safety within 4 hours.

5
Build the evidence case

Compiles a structured case — signals, context, history — for the right human team.

6
Humans decide

GameGuard investigates; people decide. It never declares guilt and never suspends anyone.

Data engineering

BattleSphere is a fictional multiplayer gaming company. Every player, message, account, transaction, and moderation case is synthetic — no real players, no real children, no real conversations. The corpus covers grooming sequences, scam scripts, harassment, fraud transactions, and adversarial cases (deliberate misspellings, emoji-coded rapport, multi-message slow burns) plus benign trash talk that must not escalate.

Architecture

LayerWhat it does
OrchestratorPlans the investigation, streams plan + tool calls to the trace panel.
Signal detectorsLinks, personal-info requests, grooming patterns, harassment, fraud — scored as evidence.
Context engineEmojis, slang, coded language, three languages, cross-conversation correlation with transaction history.
Escalation policyTwo-tier: P4 logged trash talk vs P2 trust & safety with credibility anchors (4h SLA).
Case builderStructured evidence case for human moderators, fraud analysts, child-safety specialists.
Human decisionNo auto-suspension, no guilt verdicts — investigates only.
Web UIapp.py over stdlib http.server; fully deterministic runs.

Implementation

  • Deterministic: stdlib only, offline, no API keys — identical runs; TEST-LOG.md records the determinism gate, TEST-LOG-EDGE.md a 20-case adversarial battery (passes).
  • Honest boundaries: designed as a demonstration of AI-assisted trust & safety, inspired by real industry challenges — not a claim that GameGuard is deployed by Supercell.

Technical details

  • Run it: python3 app.py, then open http://localhost:8007 in Chrome and click Run live demo (binds 127.0.0.1, port 8007).
