AGENTIC AI WORKSHOP

AI agents that do the work.

Not chatbots that answer questions — agents that read, reason, and act. These nine demos are the hands-on core of the Agentic AI Workshop: each one takes a real-world workflow, breaks it into steps, and lets an AI agent run it end to end. Every demo is open source — read the code, run it, and make it your own.

From AI That Answers to AI That Takes Action

Imagine an AI that doesn't just answer your questions, but helps you get things done. For example, instead of simply telling a health insurance company which patients may need medical attention, it could help identify those patients, decide who to contact first, prepare a personalized message, and coordinate the next steps — with people overseeing important decisions. That's the idea behind agentic AI: AI systems that can break a problem into smaller steps, use information and tools, make decisions, and take action toward a goal.

In this workshop, you'll explore nine real-world-inspired projects across healthcare, restaurants, aviation, music, equipment maintenance, gaming safety, and hiring. Each demo shows how AI agents can solve a different problem, from helping patients get the care they need to finding better restaurant locations and reviewing resumes. These projects are built primarily with Python and use simple, understandable workflows to demonstrate how an agent works — from understanding a problem to recommending or taking the next step. The examples use synthetic or local data, so you can explore how the systems work without connecting to real business systems. You don't need to be an AI expert to get started. Whether you're a student, developer, business professional, or simply curious about AI, these demos will help you understand what agentic AI is, why it matters, and how you can start building your own agents.

Stop Sending Generic Messages. Start Driving Member Action.

Run locally Source code available Prototype using synthetic data

Health plans don't just need to reach members—they need to motivate them to act. See how AI agents can personalize outreach, select appropriate communication channels, and coordinate follow-ups to help close care gaps.

How it works
Five chained agents — identity check → clinical-risk scoring (0–100) → channel selection → care-plan assembly, each with one input/output contract.
Status
Runs locally — python3 app.py → localhost:8001
Data
Fictional — 3 synthetic patients in a local file; no network calls.
Human oversight
Urgent symptoms escalate to a clinician/911 handoff before scoring; a privacy guard blocks other patients’ data.

Demo prototype — not a validated clinical system. The patients are fictional and the clinical-risk scoring is illustrative; this is built for learning how care agents work, not for making care decisions. In any real deployment, every decision that touches a member’s care must be made or reviewed by a qualified clinician — the demo’s clinician/911 escalation for urgent symptoms is the floor, not the standard.

Real-world signal: Aetna reported an 80%+ increase in overall member engagement with its enhanced RCS messaging experience. Source: CVS Health ↗

A Nurse Can’t Visit Every Member. Who Should Get the Visit First?

Run locally Source code available Prototype using synthetic data

Use predictive analytics and agentic AI to prioritize members for in-home assessments, uncover potential care gaps, and coordinate follow-up—helping health plans direct limited clinical resources where they may matter most.

How it works
One orchestrator runs six stages — member identification → outreach and scheduling → assessment interpretation → care-gap detection → follow-up coordination → program performance.
Status
Runs locally — python3 app.py → localhost:8002 (stdlib only, fully offline)
Data
Synthetic — 1,000 generated members; 265 identified for outreach on need-based criteria.
Human oversight
Deterministic guardrails at every consequential step; missing data is marked unresolved and re-checked, then closed by a human.

Demo prototype — not a validated clinical system. All 1,000 members are generated, and the assessment interpretations and care-gap flags are illustrative, not clinical guidance. Real in-home assessment programs need licensed clinicians, plan policies, and regulatory compliance; the demo keeps a human in the close-out loop as a design pattern, not as certification.

Real-world proof: Signify Health disclosed that its analytics platform identifies high-priority members for in-home evaluations. It reported that, in 2019, more than 90% of evaluated members followed up with a primary care visit.

A $7 Profit per Passenger. One Bad Forecast Can Change the Economics of a Flight.

Run locally Source code available Prototype using synthetic data

Airlines must price perishable inventory in a market that never stands still. AeroYield AI combines demand forecasts, competitor fares, seasonality, major events, fuel costs, and market signals to recommend pricing and inventory decisions that support revenue and profitability.

How it works
Observe (demand and competitor signals) → Analyze (forecast, revenue arithmetic) → Decide (candidate fares scored transparently) → Act (staged to an approval queue) → Verify (72-hour check).
Status
Runs locally — python3 app.py → localhost:8004 (offline)
Data

Human oversight
Unusual market conditions are flagged for analyst approval; the agent never touches a live booking system.

Real-world proof: IATA reports the global aviation profit margin is razor thin — just 3.7%, or a little above $7 per passenger per segment — which is why pricing and revenue management are, in IATA’s words, “the heartbeat of an airline.” Source: IATA ↗

Bad Actors Don’t Always Use Bad Words. Can AI Recognize the Pattern?

Run locally Source code available Prototype using synthetic data

From suspicious links and requests for personal information to manipulative conversations with young players, harmful behavior can hide behind emojis, slang, misspellings, and seemingly innocent messages. GameGuard AI analyzes messages and conversation context to flag potential grooming, scams, violent threats, and fraud for appropriate review.

How it works
Reads messages in full context (history, slang, misspellings, 3 languages) → scores signals, not verdicts → correlates across conversations and transactions → recommends an intervention.
Status
Runs locally — python3 app.py → localhost:8007 (offline)
Data
Synthetic — fictional “BattleSphere” gaming company; every player, message and transaction is fictional.
Human oversight
Signals, not verdicts — people decide; the system never auto-suspends, and benign trash talk is logged, not escalated.

Demo prototype for learning — not a production safety system. BattleSphere, its players, messages, and transactions are all fictional. Real safety and moderation systems need human reviewers, clear escalation paths, and ongoing evaluation: this demo surfaces signals for a person to judge — a person must decide.

Real-world proof: Supercell's games reach around 290 million monthly active players — a community scale that makes player safety a massive operational challenge, which is why Supercell's Trust & Safety team applies machine learning to detect and prevent player harm. Source: Databricks Summit ↗

Your Resume Gets 7 Seconds. Make Every One Count.

Live in browser Run locally Source code available

Job seekers send resumes into a black box and never learn why they hear nothing back. Resume Reviewer AI scores your resume against 33 hiring-manager rules and the actual job description — so you fix what matters before you hit apply.

How it works
Ingest and validate (name, LinkedIn URL, JD, PDF/DOCX) → extract text and layout in your browser → run 33 hiring-manager rules plus JD skill coverage → score with evidence and a concrete fix per failure.
Status
Runs in your browser — no backend; your resume never leaves your device.
Data
Your real uploads — PDF/DOCX parsed locally with PDF.js and Mammoth.js.
Human oversight
Advisory score, not a hiring guarantee; scanned PDFs are rejected with a clear message instead of scoring garbage.

Advisory tool, not a hiring decision-maker. The 33 rules encode one builder’s set of hiring-manager heuristics — they are not a validated selection standard. Never use the score to screen real candidates without human review; a human must verify.

Real-world signal: Ladders’ 2018 eye-tracking study found recruiters spend an average of just 7.4 seconds on the initial resume screen. Source: HR Dive ↗

How to run

No IT background needed — if you can open a web browser, you can run these. Each demo opens as a website on your own computer: you click through the story in Chrome, no coding required. Nothing is installed, no accounts, no API keys. The whole thing takes about 5 minutes the first time.

Two ways to run

Option A — Just one demo

Pick a demo below, run its three commands, and open the address in Chrome. Each demo's exact commands and port are listed in its block.

git clone https://github.com/ynpreet/<demo-repo>.git cd <demo-repo> && python3 app.py open http://localhost:<port> in Chrome

Option B — All nine at once

One command from the agentic-ai-demos repo clones anything you're missing and starts every demo on ports 8001–8009. Open any address — only the port number changes. Ctrl+C stops everything.

git clone https://github.com/ynpreet/agentic-ai-demos.git cd agentic-ai-demos && python3 run_all_demos.py open http://localhost:8001 … http://localhost:8009 in Chrome

Ports — 8001 Care · 8002 HHV · 8003 Restaurant · 8004 AeroYield · 8005 Music · 8006 FixMate · 8007 GameGuard · 8008 GuestFirst · 8009 Resume Reviewer

No git? Open any demo's GitHub page below, click the green <> Code button → Download ZIP, unzip it, open a terminal in that folder, and run python3 app.py (keep the terminal open). Then open its http://localhost:<port> address in Chrome. Prefer the classic terminal version? Run python3 demo.py instead of app.py — same story, text only.

Building your own agent? Start here.

These are the do's and don'ts distilled from teams shipping agents in production — the practices that separate demos that impress from systems that survive real users. General builder guidance, not a rulebook: use it as a pre-flight check before you build, and again before you ship. The full version covers problem definition, agent design, safety, testing, and operations.

The Agentic AI Builder's Checklist

5 sections, 44 one-line rules: before you build, designing the agent, safety & trust, testing & evaluation, shipping & operating. Keep it next to your keyboard.

Download the full checklist (PDF)

✓ Do

  • Write down the exact task the agent performs, in one sentence, before touching any code.
  • Start with the simplest thing that could work: one model call, one tool, a fixed sequence of steps.
  • Design a few clear, well-named tools instead of dozens of vague ones.
  • Decide up front which decisions the agent may make alone and which need a human's approval.
  • Put a human checkpoint before any irreversible action: sending, paying, deleting, publishing.
  • Treat user-provided content as untrusted data — never let it become instructions the agent follows blindly.
  • Build 10–15 realistic test scenarios (including failure cases) and run them after every change.
  • Track cost and latency per task from day one — agentic loops multiply both.

✗ Don't

  • Don't build an agent for a task you can't describe in plain words.
  • Don't hand the agent full autonomy on day one — earn it with evidence.
  • Don't let the agent act on text it read from the open web as if it were your instruction.
  • Don't grant the agent broader permissions than the task needs.
  • Don't ship without a way to turn the agent off quickly.
  • Don't evaluate on two perfect demo inputs and call it tested.
  • Don't launch to all users with the same settings you used in testing.
  • Don't ignore cost until the invoice arrives — set budgets and alerts.
