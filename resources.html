04

Mock Interview Questions

Interview practice across SQL, Python, Spark, Databricks, data modeling, system design and real-world scenarios.

SQLFind the second-highest salary in each department — without using LIMIT or OFFSET.

What a good answer covers: use DENSE_RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY department ORDER BY salary DESC) in a CTE, then filter for rank = 2. DENSE_RANK (not ROW_NUMBER) handles ties: if two people share the top salary, both are rank 1 and the next distinct salary is rank 2. Window functions run after WHERE/GROUP BY, which is why the ranking has to happen in a subquery or CTE.

Apache SparkWhat’s the difference between a narrow and a wide transformation, and why should you care?

What a good answer covers: narrow transformations (map, filter) compute per partition with no data movement; wide ones (groupBy, join, repartition) force a shuffle — data moves across the network between executors. Fewer wide stages usually means a faster job: reduce before shuffling (map-side combine), avoid skewed keys, and prefer broadcast joins for small tables.

Data modelingExplain the medallion (bronze / silver / gold) architecture and the quality rules you’d apply at each layer.

What a good answer covers: bronze is raw, append-only data as it arrived — no quality rules beyond capturing it. Silver is cleaned, deduplicated and typed: enforce not-null keys, valid ranges and a consistent grain here. Gold is aggregated, business-ready data with contract tests (row counts, freshness SLAs, reconciliation against silver). Each layer narrows who can depend on it.

PythonHow would you process a 50 GB CSV on a machine with 8 GB of RAM?

What a good answer covers: never load it all — stream in chunks (e.g. read_csv with chunksize, or a generator), aggregate per chunk and combine partial results. For joins or group-bys that don’t fit, use disk-backed tools (DuckDB, Dask, Spark) or a two-pass sort-merge. The principle: bound your memory by the chunk, not the file.

OrchestrationWhat makes an Airflow pipeline idempotent, and why does it matter?

What a good answer covers: re-running a task for the same date produces the same result — no duplicates, no partial writes. Achieve it with deterministic task boundaries (one logical date = one partition) and upserts or delete-and-rewrite on the target partition instead of in-place appends. It matters because Airflow retries and backfills constantly; a non-idempotent task doubles your data the first time it retries.

ScenarioTell me about a data quality incident you found in production. How did you detect it, and what guardrails did you add?

What a good answer covers: interviewers want structure — detection (dashboard, alert, or a downstream complaint), root cause (upstream schema change, late data, broken join), impact (who saw wrong numbers), and prevention: a data-quality check (dbt tests, Great Expectations, or a simple row-count/freshness alert) wired into the pipeline so it fails loudly next time. Close with what you’d monitor, not just what you fixed.
